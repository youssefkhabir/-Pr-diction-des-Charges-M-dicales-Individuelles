Engineering & Research Implementation Plan

Econometric & Machine Learning Modeling for Individual Medical Charges Prediction

A rigorous 7-phase empirical pipeline leveraging Ordinary Least Squares, L1/L2 Regularization (Ridge, Lasso, ElasticNet), Stepwise Backward Elimination, and Residual Diagnostics.

Institution: Master ISI
Course: Machine Learning / Regression
Target Domain: Healthcare Actuarial Analytics
Dataset: medical-charges.csv ($N=1,339$)
Primary Metric: Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) & $R^2$
Abstract & Project Mission

This document establishes the comprehensive technical and methodological specification for predicting individual annual medical insurance charges ($Y = \text{charges}$) conditioned on biometric, demographic, and lifestyle covariates ($X$). Adhering to the standard CRISP-DM / academic Data Science lifecycle, the study executes a full sequence: in-depth exploratory data analysis (identifying key interaction phenomena such as $\text{BMI} \times \text{smoker}$), leak-free preprocessing, benchmark parametric modeling with linear regularization, diagnostic residual audits, hypothesis-driven feature selection via Backward Elimination, and parsimonious model validation.

1. Dataset Specification & Schema Topology

The underlying dataset medical-charges.csv reflects individual historical healthcare claims across various residential zones in the United States. It comprises 1,339 observations across 7 core variables (6 independent exogenous features and 1 continuous continuous target variable).

Variable Statistical Type Description & Operational Definition Permissible Values / Domain Actuarial Relevance
age Numerical (Int) Age of the primary insurance beneficiary 18 – 64 years Strong natural baseline risk factor for chronic morbidity.
sex Categorical (Binary) Biological sex of the beneficiary female, male Demographic baseline for statistical parity assessment.
bmi Numerical (Float) Body Mass Index: $\text{weight (kg)} / [\text{height (m)}]^2$ Continuous: 15.96 – 53.13 Clinical indicator of underweight, normal, overweight, and obesity.
children Numerical (Discrete) Number of dependents covered under the policy 0 – 5 children Family coverage tier and pediatric care liability.
smoker Categorical (Binary) Regular tobacco smoking status yes, no Primary single driver of high-tier respiratory & cardiovascular claims.
region Categorical (Nominal) US residential geographic sector southwest, southeast, northwest, northeast Regional healthcare pricing variation and state-level policy differentials.
charges Numerical (Target) Billed medical costs billed to health insurance Continuous: \$1,121.87 – \$63,770.43 Endogenous dependent outcome variable ($y$). Highly right-skewed.

2. Methodological Lifecycle Workflow

The project is structured into seven tightly decoupled phases executed sequentially. The output of each phase forms the audited prerequisite for the subsequent phase.

Stage 01 Phase 1 — Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
Prerequisite to any feature manipulation

Primary Objective: Formulate an empirical, multi-angle intuition of data distributions, correlations, clustering patterns, and anomalies prior to applying any mathematical mutations.

  • 1.1 Initial Ingestion & Integrity Inspection:
    • Execute df.head(), df.tail(), and inspect dimensionality via df.shape.
    • Audit memory footprint, nullity, and type alignment via df.info().
    • Derive parametric and non-parametric summary metrics (mean, std, median, 25th/75th percentiles) via df.describe().
  • 1.2 Univariate Distributional Auditing:
    • Analyze continuous densities for age, bmi, children, and charges using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) histograms overlaid with boxplots to gauge skewness and kurtosis.
    • Examine category cardinality and balance for sex, smoker, and region via countplots.
  • 1.3 Bivariate Interaction Studies:
    • Scatter plots of continuous covariates against charges.
    • Categorical boxplots assessing variance across subgroups (e.g., charges ~ smoker, charges ~ region).
    • Critical Interaction Analysis: Cross-stratification of $\text{BMI} \times \text{smoker}$ on charges to highlight the compounding actuarial risk of obese tobacco users ($BMI > 30 \land smoker = \text{"yes"}$).
  • 1.4 Multivariate Structure & Collinearity Mapping:
    • Construct Pearson correlation matrix heatmap across numerical features.
    • Generate pairwise scatter matrices (sns.pairplot) color-coded by the smoker dimension.
  • 1.5 Outlier Identification & Interquartile Range (IQR) Audits:
    • Formulate lower and upper bounds using Tukey's fences: $[Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}, Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}]$.
    • Document extreme charges and identify whether they represent legitimate high-cost medical catastrophes or measurement artifacts.
Deliverables & Artifacts:
Matplotlib/Seaborn visualization figures with formal captions, followed by textual Markdown interpretation cells presenting clear empirical hypotheses.
Stage 02 Phase 2 — Cleaning & Leak-Free Preprocessing
Data Sanitation & Vectorization

Primary Objective: Transform raw observational data into mathematically clean, standardized numeric matrices ready for gradient- or normal-equation-based regressors, without introducing data leakage.

  • 2.1 Missing Value Diagnostics:
    • Audit total nulls using df.isnull().sum().
    • Formalize an imputation (median/mean) or deletion strategy depending on missingness mechanism (MCAR, MAR, MNAR).
  • 2.2 Exact Duplicate Detection & Deduplication:
    • Enumerate identical rows using df.duplicated().sum().
    • Purge exact duplicates (typically 1 duplicate entry in the standard insurance dataset) to prevent sample inflation.
  • 2.3 Categorical Encoding & Theoretical Justification:
    • Binary encoding for sex (0: female, 1: male) and smoker (0: no, 1: yes).
    • One-Hot Encoding (OHE) for nominal multi-class feature region, dropping the reference dummy (drop_first=True) to avoid the dummy variable trap (perfect multicollinearity).
    • Academic justification document contrasting One-Hot vs. Label Encoding for non-ordinal features.
  • 2.4 Feature Scaling & Normalization:
    • Compare Z-score standardization (StandardScaler: $\mu=0, \sigma=1$) versus Min-Max scaling (MinMaxScaler: $[0, 1]$).
    • Anti-Leakage Rule: Fit scaler exclusively on the training partition ($X_{train}$), and only transform testing data ($X_{test}$).
  • 2.5 Train/Test Partitioning:
    • Isolate exogenous matrix $X$ and endogenous vector $y = \text{charges}$.
    • Execute 80% / 20% stratified or pseudo-random split via train_test_split with an explicitly seeded random_state=42 for scientific reproducibility.
Deliverables & Artifacts:
Cleaned training/testing matrices ($X_{train}, X_{test}, y_{train}, y_{test}$), accompanied by a markdown table documenting each preprocessing transformation and its econometric justification.
Stage 03 Phase 3 — Parametric & Regularized Modeling
OLS, Ridge, Lasso, ElasticNet

Primary Objective: Implement four linear estimators spanning unregularized Ordinary Least Squares and regularized shrinks to mitigate overfitting and manage coefficient sensitivity.

# Model Class Objective Function / Penalty Behavioral Characteristic
1 Linear Regression (OLS) $\min_{\beta} \|y - X\beta\|_2^2$ Unbiased baseline; vulnerable to collinearity and variance explosion.
2 Ridge Regression (L2) $\min_{\beta} \|y - X\beta\|_2^2 + \alpha \|\beta\|_2^2$ Shrinks weights continuously toward zero ($\alpha \neq 0$); retains all features.
3 Lasso Regression (L1) $\min_{\beta} \|y - X\beta\|_2^2 + \alpha \|\beta\|_1$ Promotes sparsity; drives non-salient feature weights strictly to zero.
4 ElasticNet $\min_{\beta} \|y - X\beta\|_2^2 + \alpha \rho \|\beta\|_1 + \frac{\alpha(1-\rho)}{2}\|\beta\|_2^2$ Balanced convex combination of L1 and L2 penalty controlled by l1_ratio ($\rho$).
  • 3.1 Model Fitting & Convergence: Train all four models on the sanitized training set.
  • 3.2 Out-of-Sample Inference: Generate predicted charges $\hat{y}_{test}$ on the unseen test partition.
  • 3.3 Coefficient Interpretability: Tabulate and compare learned feature weights $\beta_j$ across models to observe shrinkage dynamics.
  • 3.4 K-Fold Cross-Validation: Apply 5-fold cross-validation (cross_val_score) across training folds to verify stability.
  • 3.5 Hyperparameter Optimization (Optional/Recommended): Systematic grid search via GridSearchCV, RidgeCV, and LassoCV to identify optimal regularization parameter $\alpha^*$.
Deliverables & Artifacts:
Trained Scikit-Learn model objects, predicted value arrays, and a structured coefficient comparison table illustrating regularization effects.
Stage 04 Phase 4 — Rigorous Model Evaluation & Residual Diagnostics
Statistical Goodness-of-Fit

Primary Objective: Evaluate the out-of-sample predictive fidelity of each candidate model using standardized regression metrics, followed by rigorous econometric validation of Gauss-Markov residual assumptions.

Metric Mathematical Definition Domain Interpretation
RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) $\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}$ Penalizes large errors heavily; expressed in original dollar units (\$).
MAE (Mean Absolute Error) $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n |y_i - \hat{y}_i|$ Robust median-like deviation; average magnitude of prediction error in \$.
$R^2$ (Coeff. of Determination) $1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}$ Proportion of variance in medical charges explained by the covariates.
Adjusted $R^2$ $1 - \left[\frac{(1 - R^2)(n - 1)}{n - p - 1}\right]$ Adjusts $R^2$ for the number of features ($p$); penalizes superfluous inputs.
MAPE (Optional) $\frac{100\%}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left|\frac{y_i - \hat{y}_i}{y_i}\right|$ Mean percentage relative deviation across claims.
  • 4.1 Quantitative Metric Compilation: Compute all four metrics across all trained estimators on identical test sets.
  • 4.2 Comparative Evaluation Matrix: Format into a clean Pandas DataFrame for side-by-side inspection.
  • 4.3 Residual Diagnostic Plots:
    • Residual Distribution Histogram + KDE (assessing normality of errors $\epsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$).
    • Residuals vs. Fitted Values Scatter Plot (evaluating homoscedasticity vs. heteroscedastic fan shapes).
  • 4.4 Actual vs. Predicted Plots: Scatter plots of $y_{test}$ vs. $\hat{y}_{test}$ with a 45-degree identity reference line ($y = x$).
  • 4.5 Academic Interpretation: Synthesize results, explain non-linear residual patterns, and identify the top-performing baseline model.
Deliverables & Artifacts:
Consolidated evaluation DataFrame, publication-grade residual plots, and analytical critique of model assumptions.
Stage 05 Phase 5 — Feature Selection & Model Parsimony
Dimension Reduction

Primary Objective: Eliminate statistically redundant or insignificant covariates to achieve a parsimonious model that reduces variance, avoids collinearity, and enhances interpretability.

  • 5.1 Stepwise Backward Elimination (Hypothesis-Testing Approach):
    • Initialize regression model with the full set of predictor features using statsmodels.api.OLS.
    • Calculate individual two-tailed p-values for Student t-tests: $H_0: \beta_j = 0$ vs. $H_1: \beta_j \neq 0$.
    • Identify the feature with the highest p-value. If $p > \alpha_{\text{threshold}} = 0.05$, remove it and refit.
    • Repeat iteratively until all remaining features achieve $p \le 0.05$. Document every step in a transition log.
  • 5.2 Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE — Optional Scikit-Learn Route):
    • Instantiate sklearn.feature_selection.RFE with a LinearRegression core estimator.
    • Isolate the optimal feature subset based on cross-validated validation scoring.
  • 5.3 Lasso Sparsity Coefficient Auditing:
    • Examine the coefficient vector $\beta^{\text{Lasso}}$ obtained from Phase 3.
    • Highlight features driven to exact numerical zero ($\beta_j = 0$), comparing algorithmic L1 selection against the statistical p-value elimination.
Deliverables & Artifacts:
Step-by-step p-value iteration log table, list of preserved vs. rejected features, and mathematical exposition of the selection algorithm.
Stage 06 Phase 6 — Comparative Analysis: All Features vs. Selected Features
Empirical Trade-off Assessment

Primary Objective: Rigorously measure whether trimming features causes performance degradation or successfully preserves predictive accuracy while reducing model complexity (Occam's razor).

  • 6.1 Re-training on Restricted Feature Matrix: Retrain all 4 models (OLS, Ridge, Lasso, ElasticNet) strictly on $X_{\text{selected}}$.
  • 6.2 Metric Re-computation: Recalculate RMSE, MAE, $R^2$, and Adjusted $R^2$ on the identical test split.
  • 6.3 Multi-Index Comparison Matrix:
    Comparative Matrix SchemaPandas MultiIndex Layout
    Index: [Linear Regression, Ridge Regression, Lasso Regression, ElasticNet] Columns: ├─ All Features: [RMSE, MAE, R², Adj. R²] └─ Selected Features: [RMSE, MAE, R², Adj. R²]
  • 6.4 Visual Impact Assessment: Grouped bar charts contrasting metrics side-by-side between feature regimes.
  • 6.5 In-Depth Discussion: Critically analyze whether Adjusted $R^2$ improved, confirming that discarded features were purely capturing noise.
Deliverables & Artifacts:
Consolidated comparative DataFrame, grouped performance charts, and written academic justification of the optimal model configuration.
Stage 07 Phase 7 — Synthesis, Limitations & Future Perspectives
Final Conclusions

Primary Objective: Conclude the research with domain-level actuarial takeaways, transparently document mathematical limitations of linear paradigms, and outline high-impact next steps.

  • 7.1 Comprehensive Findings Summary:
    • Declaration of the champion model across test benchmarks.
    • Global feature hierarchy: Rank predictors by normalized absolute coefficient magnitude ($|\beta_j^*|$).
  • 7.2 Key Actuarial & Healthcare Insights:
    • Tobacco Disparity: Quantify the monumental financial premium directly attributable to smoking status.

  • 7.4 Future Perspectives & Extensions:

    • Polynomial feature expansions ($BMI^2$, $BMI \times smoker$) to resolve linear underfitting.
    • Semi-supervised & transfer learning if external health claim registries are merged.

4. Mathematical Foundations & Objective Functions

Below are the explicit loss equations and statistical metrics underpinning the models and tests in this study.

1. Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Loss Function
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{OLS}}(\beta) = \frac{1}{2n} \sum_{i=1}^n \left( y_i - \left( \beta_0 + \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j x_{ij} \right) \right)^2 = \frac{1}{2n} \|y - X\beta\|_2^2$$
2. Ridge Regression ($L_2$ Regularization)
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Ridge}}(\beta) = \frac{1}{2n} \|y - X\beta\|_2^2 + \alpha \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j^2, \quad \alpha \ge 0$$
3. Lasso Regression ($L_1$ Regularization & Feature Sparsity)
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Lasso}}(\beta) = \frac{1}{2n} \|y - X\beta\|_2^2 + \alpha \sum_{j=1}^p |\beta_j|, \quad \alpha \ge 0$$
4. ElasticNet Regularization
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Elastic}}(\beta) = \frac{1}{2n} \|y - X\beta\|_2^2 + \alpha \cdot \rho \sum_{j=1}^p |\beta_j| + \frac{\alpha(1-\rho)}{2} \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j^2, \quad \rho \in [0, 1]$$
5. Adjusted Coefficient of Determination ($\bar{R}^2$)
$$\bar{R}^2 = 1 - \left[ \frac{(1 - R^2)(n - 1)}{n - p - 1} \right]$$

Where $n$ represents sample size ($n=1,339$) and $p$ denotes number of active regressors.
